Barcode Lookup (✨ PRE)

The Product Research Engine (PRE) is Newclear's barcode lookup system. Scan or type a UPC/EAN barcode and Newclear automatically fetches product information from multiple data sources — so you don't have to type product details by hand.

What PRE Does​

When you scan a UPC barcode (in the mobile app or type it in the web app), PRE:

  1. Looks up the UPC in its database
  2. Returns product data: title, brand, description, image URL, category, weight, and more
  3. Pre-fills the product creation form with this data

If the product is well-known (major retail brands, consumer electronics, books, etc.), you get a complete profile in seconds.

The 3-Layer Lookup System​

PRE uses a three-layer approach to find product data, in order of speed:

Layer 1 — Cache (Fastest)​

Newclear maintains an internal database of previously looked-up UPCs. If your UPC has been scanned by anyone on the platform before, the result is returned from cache instantly — no external API call needed.

The cache grows over time. Popular UPCs are always available from cache, making lookups faster the more the platform is used.

Layer 2 — External APIs (Fast)​

If not in cache, PRE simultaneously queries multiple product databases (barcode lookup APIs). These typically return results in 1–3 seconds for common consumer products.

The result is saved to the Newclear cache for future lookups.

Layer 3 — AI (Intelligent Fallback)​

If external APIs don't return useful results, PRE sends the UPC (and any other available context) to OpenAI to attempt enrichment. The AI scans its training data for information about that UPC.

note

The AI never guesses. If OpenAI can't confidently identify the product from the UPC, it returns nothing rather than fabricating information. The form fields will remain empty for the AI to leave blank — you fill those in manually.

Using PRE in the Mobile App​

  1. Open the Newclear mobile app
  2. Navigate to the AI Product Builder (✨ icon in the bottom navigation)
  3. On the first screen, tap the Scan icon
  4. Point your camera at the barcode
  5. The app scans and sends the UPC to PRE
  6. Product data appears in the form — review and edit if needed
  7. Continue to take photos and complete the AI Review step

👉 See Scanning Barcodes for the full mobile flow.

Typing a UPC Manually​

If scanning isn't working or you have a UPC list:

  1. Tap the UPC field in the mobile app or find the barcode field on the web product creation form
  2. Type the UPC number
  3. The lookup triggers automatically

What Data Comes Back​

A successful PRE lookup may return:

  • Title — The product name
  • Brand — Manufacturer/brand
  • Description — Product description
  • Category — Suggested category
  • Weight — Product weight
  • Image URL — A primary image (fetched and attached to the product)
  • MSRP — Manufacturer's suggested retail price (for some products)
  • UPC — Confirmed/normalized barcode

Not all fields are available for every product. PRE returns what it finds and leaves the rest blank.

When PRE Doesn't Recognize a Product​

Some products won't be in any database:

  • Handmade or custom items
  • Very old products
  • Items with damaged/non-standard barcodes
  • Private-label store brands
  • Liquidation items with repackaged barcodes

In these cases, fill in the fields manually. The product you create is still saved to Newclear's catalog for future reference.

Cache Improves Over Time​

Every successful PRE lookup is cached. This means:

  • The first scan of a UPC may take 1–3 seconds
  • Future scans of the same UPC (by you or any Newclear user) return instantly from cache
  • Over time, your team's common products become available at cache speed

Tips​

tip

PRE works best for branded consumer products with standard UPCs. For liquidation lots of return merchandise, expect a high hit rate since most retail products have proper UPCs.

tip

Even if PRE only fills in 50% of the fields, it still saves significant time vs. typing everything. A quick scan + 30 seconds of editing beats typing from scratch.
