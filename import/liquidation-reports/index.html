Liquidation P&L Reports

The Liquidation P&L Report is your financial dashboard for evaluating how each lot is performing — and how your overall liquidation business is doing. It shows revenue, profit, ROI, and recovery rates for every lot you've imported.

Accessing the Report​

  1. Go to Liquidation or Reports > Liquidation in the sidebar
  2. Click Reports or P&L Reports

Reading the Report​

The report shows metrics per Liquidation Order (lot). Here's what each metric means:

Lot Cost​

What you paid for the lot at auction. This is the number you entered during the B-Stock import (Step 4).

Revenue​

Total sales generated from products in this lot so far. Only counts completed sales.

Gross Profit​

Gross Profit = Revenue − Lot Cost

The money you've made above what you paid. Positive = profitable. Negative = still in the hole.

note

Gross Profit here is a simplified calculation — it's Revenue minus lot cost only. It doesn't account for selling fees (eBay/Amazon fees, Stripe fees), shipping costs, or labor. For a full picture, consider those in your analysis.

ROI %​

ROI % = Gross Profit ÷ Lot Cost × 100

Your return on investment as a percentage.

  • 100% ROI = You doubled your money
  • 50% ROI = You made 50% profit on your investment
  • 0% ROI = Broke even
  • Negative ROI = Lost money on this lot

Recovery % of MSRP​

Recovery % = Revenue ÷ Total MSRP × 100

How much of the original retail value you've recovered through sales. Industry benchmark for B-Stock resellers is typically 20–50% recovery. Higher-quality lots or better-condition items recover closer to 50–75%.

Sold % Bar​

A visual progress bar showing what percentage of the lot's products have sold. This helps you see which lots have stale unsold inventory that needs attention.

  • 100% sold = Lot is complete
  • 50% sold = Half the products still need to sell
  • 10% sold = Mostly unsold — may need pricing adjustments or better channel exposure

Summary Totals​

At the top or bottom of the report, summary totals across all lots:

  • Total invested — Sum of all lot costs
  • Total revenue — Sum of all lot revenues
  • Total gross profit — Total profit across all lots
  • Overall ROI % — Portfolio-level return

Evaluating a Lot​

Use the report to answer key questions:

"Is this lot profitable?" → Check Gross Profit and ROI %. Positive and >20% is typically a good result for liquidation.

"Am I recovering enough of MSRP?" → Check Recovery %. For high-quality lots, aim for 40%+. For heavy damage lots, 20% may be realistic.

"Are products actually selling?" → Check the Sold % bar. If a lot is 20% sold after 60 days, your pricing may be too high or the products need better listings.

"Which retailer produces better lots?" → Compare Recovery % across lots from different retailers (Target vs Walmart, etc.). Some retailers' return pallets consistently outperform others.

Filtering the Report​

Filter by:

  • Date range — Lots purchased in a specific period
  • Retailer — Compare lots from specific retailers
  • Status — Active, Sold Out, Archived

Tips​

tip

Review the P&L report at least monthly. Set a calendar reminder. Lots that aren't moving need price drops, better photos, or a different channel. Catching slow lots early lets you adjust before they go stale.

tip

Use the Sold % bar as a quick health check. Any lot below 30% sold after 45 days needs attention.

tip

Track your average ROI over time. If it's consistently below 20%, you may be overpaying at auction or underpricing your items. Either variable can be adjusted once you see the data.
