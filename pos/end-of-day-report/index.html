End-of-Day (EOD) Report

The End-of-Day (EOD) Report is your daily cash reconciliation tool. At the end of each business day, submit the EOD to record how much cash is in your drawer, reconcile any discrepancies, and carry forward the float to the next day.

What EOD Is For​

The EOD report:

  • Records the physical cash counted in your drawer at close
  • Compares against expected cash (all cash sales + opening float − cash refunds)
  • Documents any discrepancies with a reason
  • Provides a daily summary of sales by payment method
  • Carries forward any float amount to the next day's opening balance

Submitting the EOD​

Step 1: Count Your Cash​

At the end of the business day, physically count all cash in your drawer. Include bills and coins.

Step 2: Open the EOD Form​

  1. Go to POS or the Reports section
  2. Click End of Day or Submit EOD

Step 3: Enter Cash Amount​

Enter the total cash counted in the drawer. This includes:

  • The opening float you started the day with
  • Cash received from cash sales
  • Minus any cash you've removed (cash refunds, expenses paid from drawer)

Step 4: Review Summary​

The EOD form shows:

  • Expected Cash — Opening float + all cash sales − cash refunds − cash payouts
  • Actual Cash — What you entered
  • Discrepancy — The difference between expected and actual

If discrepancy is $0, great — your drawer balanced perfectly.

Step 5: Explain Discrepancies (If Any)​

If there's a discrepancy, you must categorize it. Select from:

Discrepancy TypeWhen to Use
ManualUnclassified difference — you know why but it doesn't fit another category
CashApp/ZelleCashApp or Zelle payment not properly recorded
Cash RefundCash refund given but not recorded in system
Layaway RefundLayaway deposit returned to customer
ExpensePaid something from the drawer (cleaning supplies, small purchase, etc.)
OtherAnything else with an explanation

Enter the amount for each discrepancy type and add a note explaining it.

Step 6: Float Carry-Forward​

Set the float — the amount you're leaving in the drawer for tomorrow's opening change. This is automatically deducted from the counted cash before calculating net cash.

Example: Counted $437.50, leaving $200 float → Net cash removed = $237.50.

Step 7: Submit with Approval PIN​

Click Submit EOD. You'll be prompted to enter an approval PIN. This ensures only authorized staff (managers/admins who know the PIN) can finalize the EOD.

The PIN is set in your EOD settings. Contact your admin if you don't know it.

EOD History​

Once submitted, each EOD is saved to history. You can:

  1. Go to Reports > EOD History (or similar)
  2. View any past EOD by date
  3. See the breakdown of sales, cash counted, discrepancies, and who approved

History cannot be edited after submission — it's a permanent record.

Tips​

tip

Submit the EOD at the same time each night. Consistency makes it easier to spot patterns (e.g., if you're always $5–10 short, you have a recurring problem to investigate).

tip

Set a float amount and stick to it. Starting each day with the same amount (e.g., $200) makes the math simpler and variance easier to spot.

warning

Don't skip submitting the EOD on busy days. Even a rough submission with a discrepancy explanation is better than no submission — without it, your cash history has a gap.
